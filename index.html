The Unit:
Leadership in Action

You are the nurse manager. Five situations are unfolding on your unit. Every decision you make has real, visible consequences for your staff, your culture, and your patients.

How This Works

The purpose of this simulation is to help you practice leadership judgment in realistic, emotionally complex situations where there may be more than one reasonable response. This is not a quiz with a single correct answer — it is a leadership reasoning activity designed to surface your instincts, challenge your assumptions, and give you concrete frameworks for the moments when leading well is harder than knowing what is right.

You will navigate five scenarios as the nurse manager of a busy Med-Surg unit. For each scenario, you will read the situation, make two decisions, and reflect on what you experienced. As you make decisions, watch your unit dashboard — your choices immediately shift four unit health metrics: staff satisfaction, team communication, trust in leadership, and patient safety climate.

The first four scenarios place you in reactive leadership situations: conflict, crisis, exclusion, and cultural breakdown. The fifth and final scenario asks you to move from reactive leadership to sustained culture-building — a skill that is just as important and far less often practiced. Pay attention to how that shift feels different.

The Framework Toolkit button in the upper right is always available. Before each scenario begins, you will also see a callout identifying the frameworks most relevant to what is ahead. Take a moment to review them — they will help you think through your decisions more carefully.

What the Colors Mean

Strong choice — The most effective leadership response for this situation
Reasonable — A valid approach, but incomplete or limited in some way
Reconsidered — This choice has significant drawbacks worth examining

All choices are revealed and explained after you select. There are no trick questions.

Your Unit: Five Situations Unfolding

You are the nurse manager of a 32-bed Med-Surg unit at a mid-sized regional hospital. It has been a demanding few months — and then some. Here is what is waiting for you.

Scenario 1
The Conflict at Huddle
Assignment Conflict

Two of your experienced nurses have erupted in front of the whole team during morning huddle. Your charge nurse is overwhelmed and texting you. You are being called in.

Scenario 2
The Nurse Who Was Dismissed
Patient Advocacy & Escalation

A staff RN calls you: she has raised a serious patient safety concern twice and been dismissed both times. She doesn't know what to do. Your response will either empower her or leave her stranded.

Scenario 3
The Traveler Nobody Welcomed
Team Inclusion & Culture

Your charge nurse reaches out: a travel nurse on the unit is being quietly excluded by the regular staff through comments, isolation, and closed-off team dynamics. She says she's fine. She isn't.

Scenario 4
A Unit Coming Apart
Culture Change & Leadership

Three RNs have resigned in six months. Two cliques have formed. Staff satisfaction is at a three-year low. Your charge nurses feel powerless. This is the hardest kind of leadership problem — systemic, slow, and yours to solve.

Scenario 5 · Capstone
Building Something That Lasts
Intentional Team Building

Three months after the all-hands meeting, things are quieter. Your director has noticed. Now she wants a formal team-building plan for next month's leadership meeting. But Rosa, one of your most respected senior nurses, is skeptical — and if Rosa disengages, the unit follows. What you build here either deepens the progress you've made or spends the goodwill you've earned.

Scenario-by-Scenario Review

Overall Leadership Pattern

Framework Toolkit

These frameworks appear throughout the simulation. Each scenario callout will tell you which ones are most relevant before you begin. Use this toolkit at any point during the game as a quick reference while making decisions.

SBAR
Situation · Background · Assessment · Recommendation
SBAR is a structured tool for communicating critical clinical information clearly and quickly, especially during escalations to providers or handoffs between care teams. It prevents important details from being lost and gives the receiver a clear picture of what is happening and what is needed.
IPASS
Illness Severity · Patient Summary · Action List · Situation Awareness · Synthesis
IPASS is a comprehensive handoff framework designed to reduce errors during care transitions. It ensures the receiving nurse or provider understands not just the current status, but what to watch for, what still needs to happen, and what could go wrong next.
DESC Script
Describe · Express · Suggest · Consequences
DESC is an assertive communication tool for addressing specific behaviors in a direct, constructive way without becoming aggressive or personal. It works well in one-on-one conversations where you need to name a problem, share its impact, offer an alternative, and explain what is at stake.
CUS
Concerned · Uncomfortable · Safety
CUS is a scripted escalation tool that uses three progressively urgent words to signal that a safety concern is not being taken seriously. Each word is a deliberate signal that requires acknowledgment, and "Safety" is the highest-level alert — it should stop the action and demand a response.
Two-Challenge Rule
A TeamSTEPPS escalation strategy
If a legitimate safety concern is dismissed the first time it is raised, the Two-Challenge Rule gives the nurse both permission and obligation to raise it a second time. If dismissed again, the nurse is expected to take a stronger action: escalating to a supervisor, activating Rapid Response, or using chain of command.
TeamSTEPPS
Team Strategies and Tools to Enhance Performance and Patient Safety
TeamSTEPPS is an evidence-based teamwork framework developed by AHRQ and the Department of Defense. It organizes team performance around four core skills: communication, leadership, situation monitoring, and mutual support — and provides specific tools for each.
Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Modes
Five approaches based on assertiveness and cooperativeness
The Thomas-Kilmann model identifies five ways people respond to conflict — competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, and accommodating. Effective leaders choose the mode that fits the situation rather than defaulting to the same style every time.
Crucial Conversations
Patterson, Grenny, McMillan & Switzler
Crucial Conversations is a model for navigating high-stakes discussions where emotions run high and opinions differ. Its core principles include creating psychological safety, separating facts from the stories we tell about them, and staying focused on what you actually want from the conversation rather than winning.
Nonviolent Communication (NVC)
Marshall Rosenberg
NVC asks speakers to separate what they observed from what they interpreted, identify the feeling that arose, connect it to an underlying need, and make a specific, actionable request — without blame, judgment, or demand. It is a tool for honest self-expression and empathetic listening simultaneously.
Carefronting
David Augsburger
Carefronting is the practice of addressing a problem directly because you care about the relationship, not despite it. It rejects both avoidance and aggression in favor of courageous, compassionate honesty — the idea that confronting an issue is itself an act of care.
